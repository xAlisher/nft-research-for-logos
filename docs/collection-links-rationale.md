# Sealed · Volume I — link selection rationale

_2026-09-03. Why these 12 Internet Archive documents, and how they were chosen. Full set + URLs: [`../experiments/sealed-collection/payloads.json`](../experiments/sealed-collection/payloads.json)._

## The curatorial thesis

The collection is an **argument for the stack, made in its content**. Logos exists for privacy, censorship-resistance, and self-sovereignty; each sealed piece is a document that embodies *why those matter*. The mechanic — a redacted sheet you **declassify** to reveal — is literal: the collector performs the act of disclosure the tech makes possible. So every link had to be a real document about **what power hides, what society suppresses, why we build this, and what is being erased right now.**

## Selection criteria (every piece clears all five)

1. **Legally safe to distribute.** Only officially *declassified* government material, *public-domain* texts, freely-published *manifestos*, or *archived formerly-public* pages. No active-classified leaks (this is why WikiLeaks was excluded — see below).
2. **Verified & durable.** Every URL was fetched and confirmed to resolve; all live on the Internet Archive / Wayback Machine, which is itself a preservation-against-censorship institution — the payload won't rot.
3. **Thematic fit.** Each maps to a Logos value (surveillance, censorship, sovereignty, erasure).
4. **Balance.** Four categories, spread across four centuries (1644 → 2017) and four *kinds* of hiding — state secrets, banned art, foundational ideas, memory-holed records.
5. **Recognizability.** Each is historically weighty enough to teach something on reveal.

## The four categories (why each earns its place)

**1. Declassified government documents — "what power hides" (5 pieces).**
The state's own secrets, exposed. *Church Committee* (mass-surveillance abuse, the birth of intelligence oversight), *MKUltra* and *CIA "Family Jewels"* (illegal domestic operations), *Pentagon Papers* (the war the public was lied to about), *SSCI Torture Report* (post-9/11 interrogation). Together they are the empirical case for *why* citizens need protection from the surveilling state — the core Logos premise.

**2. Banned / censored texts — "what society suppressed" (3 pieces).**
*Areopagitica* (1644) is the keystone: Milton's foundational argument against censorship, itself published in defiance of a licensing order — the perfect Nº 1. *Ulysses* and *Lady Chatterley's Lover* were criminalized as obscene for decades; their reversal is how free expression was won in law. Censorship of ideas, made concrete.

**3. Cypherpunk / sovereignty canon — "why we build this" (2 pieces).**
*A Cypherpunk's Manifesto* (Hughes, 1993 — "privacy is necessary for an open society in the electronic age") and *A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace* (Barlow, 1996). The intellectual lineage Logos descends from — the collection naming its own ancestors.

**4. Erased Wayback snapshots — "what is being erased now" (2 pieces).**
*EPA Climate Change* (scrubbed 2017) and *White House Climate Change* (removed at the 2017 transition). Present-tense censorship: official pages memory-holed from live government sites, surviving only because the Archive preserved them. This is the sharpest, most current beat — "we kept what they deleted" — and it showcases the Wayback Machine as the same preservation ethic Logos encodes in protocol.

## Why not WikiLeaks

WikiLeaks hosts *active-classified* material; an official Logos/IFT collection linking to it carries real legal + brand + political exposure and would require Eric (legal) + leadership sign-off. The Internet Archive set delivers the same "declassify to reveal" education with none of that risk, and is arguably *more* on-brand (the Archive is a preservation-against-erasure institution). WikiLeaks remains a separate escalation, not acted on here.

## Suggested ordering & extensibility

Open with **Areopagitica** (Nº 1 — the anti-censorship keystone), close the volume on the **erased climate pages** (the present-tense gut-punch). Future **volumes** can theme deeper (surveillance-only, banned-books-only, a nation's erased archive) — the mechanic is content-agnostic, so scaling is just more verified links.

## Honesty
Testnet, educational, no real value. Links are public Internet Archive items; the collection curates and contextualizes them, it does not host or alter them.
